HF propagation is one of the things that makes amateur radio feel magical. Understanding how radio waves interact with the ionosphere is the key to working stations thousands of miles away.

## The Ionosphere

The ionosphere contains several regions commonly described as the D, E, F1, and F2 layers. These ionized regions can refract radio waves back toward Earth. The F2 layer, reaching roughly 200–400 kilometers in altitude, is especially important for long-distance HF communication.

## Solar Influence

Solar activity drives ionization. During solar maximum, which follows an approximately 11-year cycle, the higher HF bands such as 10, 12, and 15 meters can support worldwide communication. During solar minimum, lower bands such as 40, 80, and 160 meters are often more dependable for DX.

## Practical Tips

- 20 meters is dependable year-round during daylight hours.
- 40 meters provides useful regional coverage day and night, with better DX after dark.
- A solar flux index above 100 generally favors the higher bands.
- Tools such as VOACAP, PSKReporter, and DX clusters can help identify openings.
