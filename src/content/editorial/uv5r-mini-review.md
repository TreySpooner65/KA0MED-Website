Is the Baofeng UV-5R Mini just a novelty, or does this shrunken-down handheld deserve a spot in your everyday-carry setup or emergency go-kit? Let’s break it down.

## Size

The “Mini” name is not just marketing. Without the antenna, the radio measures roughly 4.3 × 2.0 × 1.5 inches and weighs about 200 grams, or 7.1 ounces, with the battery attached. It feels compact while still having a solid, durable feel instead of coming across like a cheap plastic toy.

Despite the smaller footprint, it includes a sharp 1.44-inch color display that clearly shows dual-band frequencies. For operators who prefer a more technical appearance, it is also available in a transparent “Quartz” version that exposes the internal circuit board.

## Bands

The UV-5R Mini offers standard dual-band transmit capability with rated power levels of 5 watts, 2 watts, and 1 watt. It transmits on the 2-meter and 70-centimeter amateur bands.

It also provides expanded receive-only coverage, including:

- FM broadcast radio from 76–108 MHz
- Airband reception
- 300–390 MHz reception
- NOAA weather channels
- Additional VHF and UHF coverage

## Build Quality

Build quality is surprisingly good for such a small handheld. The body feels sturdy, the controls are responsive, and the color display gives it a more modern feel than older Baofeng models.

The menu system is still recognizably Baofeng, so new users should expect a learning curve. Once the layout becomes familiar, however, it is manageable.

## Strengths

- USB-C charging
- Wireless programming
- Compact size
- Excellent value
- Good-quality airband reception

## Weaknesses

- Transmit coverage is limited to the 2-meter and 70-centimeter bands
- It retains the compromises associated with budget Baofeng radios
- Measured power output was well below the advertised 5 watts

## Technical Data

| Specification | Value |
| --- | --- |

## Verdict

The UV-5R Mini is more than a novelty. It is small, affordable, and capable enough to make sense as an EDC radio, backup handheld, or emergency go-kit radio.

It will not replace a higher-end amateur radio, and its real-world output does not live up to the advertised 5-watt figure. For its size and price, however, it offers substantial value.

**Signal Score: 8.0/10**
